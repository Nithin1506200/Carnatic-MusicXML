# 1. Training and Sight-Reading

This section defines the practice order for reading Carnatic notation on alto
saxophone. The canonical instrument and notation rules remain in
`carnatic_saxophone.md`.

## Recommended practice order

1. Establish a steady written `Sa = G` with a drone at concert `Bb`.
2. Read middle-octave swaras without fingering hesitation.
3. Add lower-octave swaras (dot below plus italic labels) and upper-octave
   swaras (overdot plus bold labels).
4. Practise sustained notes with an even tone and controlled breath.
5. Read one-beat and half-beat swaras against a metronome.
6. Practise sarali varisai, janta varisai, dhatu varisai, upper-sthayi
   exercises, and alankaras.
7. Read tala groupings while counting the angas aloud or with hand gestures.
8. Increase tempo only after pitch, fingering, articulation, and tala are
   stable.

## Sight-reading rules

- Read the raga, arohana, avarohana, tala, eduppu (if present), and tempo
  before playing.
- Identify the written tonic and accidental set; do not assume every score uses
  the project default.
- Scan for octave changes, long phrases, rests, repeated patterns, and tala
  boundaries.
- Keep tala continuous through rests and breaths.
- Take breaths at phrase boundaries where possible; never insert a breath that
  changes the tala count.
- Start slowly enough to avoid stopping. Repeat with the same pulse before
  raising the tempo.
- Treat gamaka markings as musical instructions, not decoration. When the
  source does not specify a gamaka, do not invent one as authoritative.
